Glossary and notation

ALD
Atomic layer deposition: sequential surface reactions used to grow films. Conformality and growth per cycle depend on chemistry and geometry. Chapter 2.
Polarization P; displacement D
P is dipole moment per volume, C/m². D includes polarization and background dielectric response. The decomposition must match the model convention; do not count background susceptibility twice. Chapter 3.
Ferroelectric; antiferroelectric-like response
A ferroelectric supports switchable polarization states. A double-loop or antiferroelectric-like response in fluorite-derived films requires a specific structural/electrical explanation rather than automatically importing one microscopic model. Chapter 2.
Remanent and saturation polarization
Remanence Pᵣ is the branch polarization at zero internal field under specified history. Saturation Pₛ is the limiting switchable component in the selected model; neither is a context-free material constant.
Coercive field
A switching-related field defined by a stated convention. A polarization zero crossing, displacement zero crossing, or inferred barrier field can give different values.
Domain; domain wall; depolarization
A domain has approximately common polarization orientation; a wall separates domains. Incomplete screening creates an opposing depolarizing field. Domain dimensions do not by themselves define usable memory density.
HZO, ZrO₂, BaTiO₃
Mixed hafnium–zirconium oxide; zirconium dioxide; barium titanate. Composition does not specify phase, orientation, defects, or integrated-device behavior.
IGO, IWO, WO₃₋ₓ
Gallium-doped indium oxide and tungsten-doped indium oxide are channel materials in selected studies. Oxygen-deficient tungsten oxide can play an electrode/oxygen-chemistry role. The layers have different functions.
FeFET; FeCAP
Ferroelectric field-effect transistor; ferroelectric capacitor. A capacitor loop and a transistor's current memory window are related through device electrostatics and history, not interchangeable observables.
Memory window
Separation between read states, often threshold voltages under a specified extraction criterion. Report bias, read delay, history, temperature, and distribution.
PUND
Positive-up-negative-down pulse methodology: compare switching and repeated nonswitching pulses to estimate switchable charge. Leakage/trapping mismatch can contaminate subtraction.
Wake-up; fatigue; retention; endurance
Early-cycle evolution, cycling degradation, state persistence over time, and tolerated repeated operations. Each needs a protocol and failure definition; none is determined by a static loop alone. Chapter 4.
Identifiability; calibration; validation
Identifiability asks whether observations constrain parameters or mechanisms uniquely enough. Calibration fits them. Validation tests predictions under suitable unused conditions. A good fit alone is insufficient.
SPICE; Verilog-A; RTL
SPICE solves circuit equations; Verilog-A describes analog behavior subject to simulator support; RTL describes clocked digital transfers and logic. Numerical state equations and digital behavioral memories do not automatically implement a physical research device.
ECC; BER
Error-correcting code; bit-error rate or probability with a defined population and interval. Independence and tail assumptions strongly affect predicted residual errors. Chapter 6.
Storage; working memory; cache; compute-in-memory
Persistent retention; active computation state; a managed copy for fast reuse; computation performed in or near a memory structure. Compare technologies by the actual role and access contract.
PDK; LEF; Liberty; GDS
Process design kit; physical abstract geometry; cell timing/power models; hierarchical geometric layout exchange. Abstract files alone supply no characterized timing or fabrication qualification.
DRC; LVS; PEX; STA
Design-rule checking; layout-versus-schematic comparison; parasitic extraction; static timing analysis. All require appropriate process/model support. These foundry checks are not claimed for the abstract research macro.
Prefill; decoding; KV cache
Prompt processing; sequential generation; stored keys and values used by attention. They stress compute, bandwidth, and capacity differently. Chapter 9.
TTFT; inter-token latency; throughput
Time to first token; interval between outputs for one request; aggregate outputs per second. Queueing and batching prevent substituting one for another.
Roofline; arithmetic intensity
A compute/bandwidth performance bound; useful operations per transferred byte. The modeled bandwidth must match the memory boundary being analyzed.
Pareto optimum; global optimum
A nondominated choice across competing objectives; a best feasible choice for a stated objective over a defined domain. Exhaustive enumeration proves optimality only over its enumerated choices.
Utilization ρ; PUE; joules/token
Offered service load relative to capacity; facility energy divided by IT energy over a stated interval; energy divided by useful token count. A mean queue response does not certify tail latency. Chapter 10.

Unit conventions

1 µC/cm² = 0.01 C/m². 1 MV/cm = 10⁸ V/m. 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m. 1 pJ = 10⁻¹² J. 1 MiB = 2²⁰ bytes; 1 GB/s = 10⁹ bytes/s. One byte contains eight bits. Energy density is J/m³; multiplying by active volume yields joules. A capacitance per area is F/m², not F. Use kelvin in Arrhenius factors. State whether operation counts treat a multiply-add as one operation or two.
