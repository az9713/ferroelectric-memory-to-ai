Ferroelectric Materials, Memory Devices, and AI Systems

Expanded textbook edition: foundational explanations before specialist terminology, 82 worked solutions, and a cumulative numerical laboratory. Start with the newly expanded guided introduction and paper-reading guides. What changed and how it was checked

An independent graduate-level course that uses selected publications by Prof. Asif Khan and collaborators as case studies to connect ferroelectric materials and devices with memory systems and AI workloads. Foundational explanations and engineering extensions are distinguished from the papers’ demonstrated results.

Research case-study coverage: 15 distinct Khan-coauthored papers and preprints provide substantive examples: 14 selected works from the five-year baseline and one foundational 2021 Preisach-model paper outside that window. Ten of these works appear in the seven guided-reading exercises; the others support chapter discussions. One additional Khan-coauthored paper is cited briefly for comparison, bringing the total cited to 16. These counts are separate from the 101-record publication inventory. See the claim-to-source ledger for evidence and limitations.

Understand the research behind the course: Connected synthesis of 15 selected papers · Prof. Asif Khan-only bibliography.

Start with the research overview, then use the diagnostic and prerequisite map. The central question is what changes elsewhere when you turn one materials, device, circuit, or workload knob. Every major numerical result distinguishes published evidence from teaching assumptions.

What has been built: a 101-record bounded publication baseline; 14 selected full-text assessments; primary-source links and provenance; Python projects; actual ngspice and Verilog runs; generic controller synthesis; and abstract physical views. What the evidence does not support: a calibrated complete research-device compact model, fabrication signoff, or a forecast of deployed AI-hardware performance. Requirement-by-requirement acceptance audit.

1. Semiconductor foundations

Si/Ge, bands, statistics, transport, junctions, MOS electrostatics, interfaces.

2. Materials and mechanisms

HZO, zirconia, BaTiO₃, oxide channels, symmetry, domains, processing, defects.

3. Models and identifiability

Published static equations, synthetic fitting, coercive crossings, energy, held-out checks.

4. Devices and reliability

Programming, reading, trapping, kinetics, retention, endurance, measurement history.

5. Circuits and arrays

Sensing, drivers, RC, banking, area, error constraints, Pareto tradeoffs.

6. Memory systems

Storage, working memory, caches, addressing, ECC, wear, bandwidth contracts.

7. Digital implementation

RTL and Verilog, handshake, reset, faults, testbench, synthesized controller and waveforms.

8. Chip and package integration

Thermal budgets, yield, placement, floorplan, LEF/GDS, process-support boundaries.

9. AI workloads

Prefill, decoding, weights, KV cache, precision, roofline, capacity and latency.

10. Servers and data centers

Queueing, utilization, communication, cooling, PUE, power and joules per useful token.

Capstone. Defend the chain

Bidirectional reasoning, conditional sensitivity, fixed architecture versus redesign, missing experiments.
