Prof. Asif Khan's research: from ferroelectric state to reliable memory

How materials, interfaces, sensing architectures, reliability, and AI-assisted modeling connect across a selected research portfolio.

A memory engineer is offered a new ferroelectric stack with a larger electrical hysteresis window. Should the engineer replace a conventional storage cell with it? The answer depends on whether the window remains readable after programming, waiting, repeated access, fabrication, and environmental stress. It also depends on the circuitry needed to distinguish states. The fifteen works considered here progressively expose different parts of that problem. Their connection is the effort to convert a controllable physical state into a reliable information-bearing state.

This is an independent synthesis of publications coauthored by Prof. Asif Khan and collaborators. It is not a statement of his personal roadmap, and the ordering does not imply that every later paper directly builds on every earlier one. The selected portfolio includes experimental studies, theoretical analysis, architecture reviews, and simulation methods. Their results have different evidentiary roles. Throughout, named measurements belong to their authors; cross-paper interpretations are identified as synthesis; numerical examples explicitly labeled illustrative are constructed for this course.

1. The state that a memory must preserve

A ferroelectric has an electric polarization that can be reversed by an applied field and can persist after that field is removed. Polarization, denoted $P$, is dipole moment per unit volume and has units of coulombs per square meter. A ferroelectric field-effect transistor, or FeFET, places a ferroelectric within the gate stack of a transistor. Its stored state changes the relation between gate voltage and channel current. The reader does not directly observe all the dipoles; it observes an electrical response influenced by the entire stack.

Define a threshold voltage as the gate voltage satisfying a specified channel-current criterion at a fixed drain voltage and read protocol. The memory window is the separation between threshold voltages associated with programmed states. A larger separation may help distinguish those states, but the separation is not an intrinsic material constant. It depends on polarization, trapped charges, electrostatic coupling, semiconductor response, and the time elapsed since programming. A capacitor loop and a transistor memory window are therefore different observables.

For the running case, consider a proposed laminated gate stack: hafnium–zirconium oxide, HZO, separated by an aluminum-oxide interlayer, above a semiconductor channel. “Laminated” means that distinct thin layers form the stack; HZO here denotes Hf₀.₅Zr₀.₅O₂. The immediate engineering objective is reliable multilevel storage, where more than two programmed threshold states represent several bits per cell. We do not assume that the same device simultaneously has every favorable property reported across the fifteen papers.

Let the internal state at time $t$, measured in seconds, be $x(t)=(P(t),q(t),n(t))$. Here $q$ is a signed effective areal trapped charge in C/m², and $n$ is an effective areal density of electrically active defects in m⁻². Real devices need spatial distributions, multiple trap populations, and additional variables. This three-component state is a teaching abstraction that makes the research connections visible. The applied voltage waveform $u(t)$, temperature $T(t)$ in kelvin, and fixed geometry/process descriptor $g$ determine how the state changes.

Write the state evolution and observation schematically as

$$\frac{dx}{dt}=F(x,u,T;g),\qquad V_{\mathrm{th}}=H(x,T;g).$$

The function $F$ describes switching, charge exchange, and defect evolution. The function $H$ converts the internal state into the measured threshold in volts. These functions are not a calibrated device model. They distinguish the physical dynamics from the measurement and show which missing information a paper can supply.

The extraction work constrains a relation between field and polarization. The compact-model work asks how polarization and screening jointly affect the transistor. Interlayer chemistry changes both charge dynamics and the relation between state and threshold. Domain studies examine spatial degrees of freedom suppressed in our three-variable description. Reliability studies test the evolution of the state under specific histories. Architecture papers decide how to read it. The two AI papers address the cost and ambiguity of learning or evaluating the functions. This common model gives the portfolio continuity without forcing all experiments into one material platform.

The relevant system object is ultimately a distribution of observed states, not one best device. Nominally identical cells have different geometry, defects, and histories. Their threshold distributions can overlap even when their means are widely separated. As the argument moves from materials to arrays, the central question changes from “does it switch?” to “can the complete read operation infer the stored symbol at the required error rate?” That change in question explains why several apparently secondary process and reliability papers matter to the architecture.

2. Chronology and conceptual evolution

The earliest selected work, the 2021 Preisach parameter-extraction paper, provides a common language for comparing hysteresis measurements. The 2022 compact-model contribution introduces an important obstruction: a transistor cannot always be understood by coupling a ferroelectric loop directly to an ideal gate capacitor. The 2023 negative-capacitance analysis asks a related but distinct question about spatial stability. The BaTiO₃ work, first posted in 2024 and published in 2025, demonstrates why the growth process and material family remain essential even when the desired electrical function is similar.

The 2025–2026 selection broadens the engineering question. The architecture review and vertical-NAND perspective connect sensing and storage geometry. The domain, first-switch, oxidant, wake-up, thermal, radiation, and coercive-field studies investigate different constraints on creating and maintaining a useful state. The AI papers add methods for exploring the resulting coupled models. This is a conceptual development visible in the selected corpus, not evidence that all earlier questions were solved before later work began.

Nor is publication order a performance curve. The BaTiO₃ capacitor, silicon-channel FeFET, oxide-channel transistor, and laminated polycrystalline-silicon device have different geometries and protocols. Their headline voltages, cycle counts, and operating conditions cannot be placed on one improvement chart without normalizing what was tested. A valid historical inference is that the selected research increasingly exposes coupled constraints. A claim of monotonically improving manufacturable memory performance would require a comparable device series that this selection does not supply.

3. From a loop to an identifiable model

The 2021 extraction paper addresses a practical measurement problem: reported ferroelectric parameters can depend on how a hysteresis loop is interpreted. The total displacement density includes a switchable polarization contribution and a linear dielectric contribution. With internal electric field $E$ in V/m, vacuum permittivity $\varepsilon_0$ in F/m, and dimensionless background relative permittivity $\varepsilon_b$, the model writes $D=P+\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_bE$. Thus a zero crossing of $D$ need not occur at the field where $P$ crosses zero. Incomplete switching adds another ambiguity because a minor loop need not reveal the saturation values. The paper develops a modified Preisach description and parameter extraction; its Figure 2 supplies the explicitly simulated example reproduced in this course. Primary source, equations and Figure 2.

The enduring contribution to our running case is methodological. A material ranking is meaningful only after the measurement operator has been specified. If one group reports a displacement crossing and another extracts a polarization coercive field after subtracting the dielectric contribution, the two numbers may answer different questions. A fit can improve comparability, but fitting does not remove assumptions. An excellent curve reconstruction does not by itself establish a unique microscopic mechanism or a switching-time law.

The 2022 compact-model contribution makes the next step harder. The related Wang thesis describes a p-channel silicon FeFET with a 5 nm ZrO₂ layer and a 1.5 nm SiO₂ interfacial layer. A measured polarization hysteresis width and the read-current memory window differ. The model introduces a dissipative screening path to explain why polarization charge does not couple entirely into the semiconductor response. The evidence used here is thesis §3.3, PDF pages 49–54; it is not an independently acquired copy of the journal article. Device and screening analysis.

To see the consequence without claiming to reproduce that model, linearize our observation function near a specified operating state. Let $k_P$ and $k_q$ be signed coupling coefficients in m²/F. Holding geometry and temperature fixed, an illustrative first-order threshold perturbation is

$$\delta V_{\mathrm{th}}=k_P\delta P+k_q\delta q.$$

One measured voltage change supplies one equation for two unknown physical changes. If the illustrative coefficients are both 10 m²/F, an observed 0.1 V shift can arise from $\delta P=0.01$ C/m² with no charge change, or from $\delta q=0.01$ C/m² with no polarization change. Infinitely many mixtures give the same reading. The coefficients and numbers are teaching assumptions; their purpose is to exhibit non-identifiability, not to assign a measured sensitivity to any selected stack.

An extra observation helps only if it responds differently to the two unknowns. A second threshold measurement with precisely proportional sensitivities repeats the same information. A matched capacitor measurement, read-delay sweep, temperature perturbation, or charge-sensitive technique can help when a justified model predicts distinct responses. The experimental-design lesson develops this general issue; the practical research implication is to seek discriminating protocols rather than merely denser samples of one curve.

This early modeling problem returns in the 2026 interfacial-charge and agentic-calibration papers. Both confront competing explanations of an electrical response. The progression is not from “simple physics” to “AI solves physics.” It is from recognizing measurement ambiguity to adding physical observations, model constraints, and tests outside the fitting conditions. The running memory decision cannot be supported by a fitted window alone, because its retention prediction depends on which internal variable produced that window.

4. Interfaces become design variables

In a simple introductory description, trapped charge is a defect to eliminate because it shifts transistor thresholds. The selected laminated-stack papers require a more specific interpretation. Charge can screen polarization, alter internal voltage division, and change switching conditions. It can also escape, accumulate asymmetrically, or create drift. Whether it helps depends on its location, sign, kinetics, and coupling to the stored state. “More trapping” and “less leakage” are therefore incomplete optimization objectives.

The ALD-oxidant study provides a particularly useful comparison. Atomic layer deposition, ALD, forms films through alternating surface reactions. The authors change the oxidant used for an Al₂O₃ insert while retaining two specified stack arrangements: a gate-side 12/3 configuration and an internal 8/3/8 configuration, with thicknesses in nanometers. Water-based deposition produces a larger reported memory window than ozone-based deposition. The retention consequence differs between arrangements: the internal insert retains its benefit over the reported test up to $10^4$ s at 125 °C, whereas the gate-side water-grown case degrades more strongly. The proposed explanation involves different leakage and charge dynamics. Process comparison and retention results.

The inference is stronger than “water gives a better film” and narrower than “leakage improves memory.” A process intervention interacts with where the resulting layer sits. The same change in charge exchange can assist switching in one arrangement and undermine state stability in another. In the running case, specifying only the chemical formula and thickness would omit a parameter that changes the engineering recommendation. The intervention must include deposition chemistry and complete layer order.

The interfacial-charge/coercive-field preprint investigates why adding an Al₂O₃ interlayer changes more than passive capacitance. It combines chemical characterization, electrical reversal measurements, and phase-field modeling to argue for defect-mediated charge dynamics and a state-dependent internal bias. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy probes chemical environments; first-order reversal curves probe history-dependent switching; phase-field modeling represents spatially varying order and electrostatics. The agreement among these views supports a mechanism interpretation, although defect populations and internal fields remain inferred through measurement and modeling assumptions. Mechanism claim and methods.

These two papers complement the screening issue raised in 2022. Screening is no longer only a correction needed to explain why a transistor deviates from an ideal capacitor. Interface design becomes a means of changing useful behavior. The important qualification is that the experimentally accessible control is a process step, not an isolated microscopic parameter. Changing an oxidant can change impurities, vacancies, barriers, and microstructure together. A causal claim that one particular defect species explains the gain needs additional evidence beyond the gain itself.

The WO₃₋ₓ/HZO study extends this theme from charge screening to phase stability and initial conditioning. “Wake-up” denotes a change, commonly an increase, in switchable polarization during early cycling. In the reported ultrathin 5 nm HZO structures, an oxygen-reservoir layer and its oxygen content substantially reduce the conditioning needed at 125 °C. The authors report a reduction from roughly $10^5$ cycles to 10 under their conditions and use first-principles calculations to interpret phase stabilization. This is evidence about a particular film stack and elevated-temperature behavior, not a universal qualification of all HZO memories. Abstract and physical interpretation.

Here the connecting variable is the distribution and movement of oxygen-related defects, but their role differs among studies. A layer that changes phase fractions is not automatically equivalent to an interface that changes tunneling charge. The useful synthesis is that interface chemistry participates in the functional state. The unsupported shortcut would be to treat all oxygen vacancies as one interchangeable control knob with the same effect across every stack.

Why a larger initial window can lose its advantage

Consider two hypothetical stacks A and B with window $W(t)$ in volts. Stack A has a stable contribution of 0.6 V and an initially helpful charge contribution of 0.6 V that relaxes with time constant $\tau=100$ s. Stack B has a stable 0.9 V window. Assume no other degradation and write

$$W_A(t)=0.6+0.6e^{-t/100},\qquad W_B(t)=0.9.$$

At programming completion A appears better: 1.2 V versus 0.9 V. Equality occurs when $e^{-t/100}=1/2$, giving $t=100\ln2\simeq69.31$ s. After 1,000 s, A is approximately 0.600027 V while B remains 0.9 V. This calculation is not a fit to the oxidant paper. It proves a smaller logical point: an initial ranking cannot determine a retained ranking when contributions have different relaxation times. A matched-delay distribution measurement is necessary before promoting initial window gain into a multilevel-memory claim.

5. Two different meanings of controlling domains

A domain is a region of approximately common polarization orientation; a domain wall separates regions. A lumped capacitor model suppresses this spatial structure. The domain-free negative-capacitance analysis asks when a homogeneous state can remain stable against the alternative of forming domains. Negative differential capacitance means that a component's incremental voltage change and charge change have opposite signs. Stability must be assessed for the complete coupled structure, including spatial perturbations. The paper identifies conditions involving layer thickness and domain-wall energy rather than treating a homogeneous solution as sufficient evidence of a realizable state. Theoretical scope.

The zirconia-domain preprint examines the other side of this design space: closely spaced charged walls and extremely small domains. Atomic imaging and calculations support a picture of charged head-to-head and tail-to-tail walls stabilized by compensation. The work distinguishes observed structures from calculated electronic and wall-motion properties. A small observed domain demonstrates an accessible structural state; it does not demonstrate electrodes that independently select, switch, and read each domain. Version 2, imaging and modeling claims.

There is no contradiction between a paper seeking to suppress domains and another investigating useful domain structures. The desired function differs. A homogeneous negative-capacitance element aims to exploit a particular collective response. A domain-wall concept may exploit spatial structure and wall dynamics. For the running storage case, the first task is to decide which physical degree of freedom encodes the symbol. Without that decision, “better domain control” does not specify an optimization problem.

A scaling argument explains why the microscopy result cannot be translated directly into bit density. If one domain were one bit, it would still require independent write selectivity, a detectable read signal, a retention barrier, and tolerable disturbance of neighbors. A domain that moves easily may reduce a switching barrier while increasing unwanted motion unless another mechanism stabilizes it. This tradeoff is a hypothesis to investigate for the proposed architecture, not evidence that the reported structures necessarily fail retention.

The BaTiO₃ kinetic-growth paper keeps this discussion from collapsing into a hafnia-only narrative. BaTiO₃ is a perovskite ferroelectric with different growth and interface constraints. The acquired preprint studies how the kinetic conditions of pulsed-laser deposition improve capacitor quality, leakage, and electrical switching. Its low-voltage and cycling results belong to epitaxial capacitors, including the reported 20 nm examples. They cannot be transplanted into an ALD HZO vertical-NAND stack. Growth-control study.

Its relevance is comparative: an apparent materials limit can include a process-induced limitation. Better interfaces can reveal useful behavior that leakage had obscured. Conversely, a low switching voltage in a carefully grown capacitor does not settle conformal fabrication inside a tall memory structure. The portfolio therefore spans two complementary questions: what physical states are possible, and what processes can realize those states in a useful device geometry?

6. Sensing determines the architecture

The polarization-sensing architecture review organizes ferroelectric memories by how stored states become readable signals. A capacitor cell can reveal switching charge; a transistor can convert a state into channel-current contrast; a tunnel structure can convert it into a resistance difference. These choices change access devices, peripheral circuits, signal margins, and whether reading alters the stored state. The review's role is to connect physical mechanisms to architectural choices. It includes literature results and proposals, which must not all be attributed to demonstrations by its coauthors. Architecture review.

The bridge from interfaces to architecture is the read contract. A destructive read may require restoration and therefore additional write endurance. A small signal may require larger capacitors or more sensitive, slower sensing. A transistor cell can amplify a state into a current difference, but channel variation and bias history enter the measurement. A storage architecture should be compared using the entire access path: selected and unselected cells, wiring, reference generation, sensing, verification, and any restoration.

The vertical-NAND perspective develops a hybrid interpretation in which polarization and trapped charge can cooperate. NAND strings connect cells along a shared channel; the selected cell must be accessed while other cells allow conduction without losing their own states. Three-dimensional stacking improves capacity per footprint but introduces voltage, geometry, process, and interference constraints. The perspective argues that ferroelectric/charge interactions may help address these constraints. Its ambitious scaling trajectories are research directions, not demonstrations of a completed many-layer commercial device. Perspective and proposed transitions.

This hybrid picture resolves an apparent inconsistency in the portfolio. Why study charge trapping if ferroelectric memory is supposed to replace charge storage? Because the useful state of the complete device can involve both polarization and charge. The engineering distinction is not a pure binary between “polarization technology” and “charge technology.” It concerns which mechanisms provide switching, screening, retention, and read separation, and how reliably those mechanisms can be controlled together.

A larger total window does not determine bits per cell

Suppose the running memory uses $L$ equally probable levels, with $L\ge2$, uniformly spaced over a total mean-to-mean span $W$. The adjacent mean separation is $d=W/(L-1)$. Assume each level has Gaussian threshold noise of standard deviation $\sigma$ volts, equal for all levels, and that decisions use midpoints between means. Define $Q(z)$ as the upper-tail probability of a standard normal random variable.

An outer level has one wrong-decision tail, of probability $Q(d/(2\sigma))$. Each interior level has two, so averaging across the $L$ levels gives the symbol error probability

$$p_{\mathrm{symbol}}=\frac{2(L-1)}{L}\,Q\!\left(\frac{W}{2\sigma(L-1)}\right).$$

This derivation assumes midpoint decisions, equal priors, identical Gaussian widths, and no drift or correlated disturbance. It predicts symbol error, not the bit error after a particular binary labeling and error-correcting code. For the illustrative values $W=1.2$ V and $\sigma=0.05$ V, four levels give about $4.75\times10^{-5}$ symbol error. Eight levels give approximately 0.0757, and sixteen give approximately 0.397. The window is unchanged, but dividing it among more levels consumes the margin rapidly.

For an interior-level error target of at most $10^{-3}$, require $2Q(d/(2\sigma))\le10^{-3}$. The inverse normal tail is approximately 3.29053, giving $d\ge6.58105\sigma$. With $\sigma=0.05$ V, eight levels require at least 2.30337 V total span and sixteen require 4.93579 V. This is an illustrative sufficient condition under the stated model, not a target extracted from the selected papers. Real read distributions and coding can change the requirement.

The architecture implication is concrete. An oxidant or interface change that increases window but also broadens distributions may yield fewer usable levels than its nominal window suggests. Conversely, a smaller mean window with tighter distributions may store more information reliably. The needed evidence is a joint distribution study at the intended delay, temperature, cycling state, and cell geometry. The architecture papers supply the motivation; the materials papers supply candidate interventions; neither substitutes for this missing compatibility test.

7. History, heat, and radiation test different parts of the state

The first-switch paper shows why an endurance number can conceal the most consequential event. In the studied silicon-channel FeFETs, the first programming event creates a large fraction of the interface-trap change accumulated before window closure. Transfer characteristics and charge-pumping measurements distinguish early interface effects from further cycling damage. The reported fractions are device- and protocol-specific. Their general lesson is that a pristine cell and a conditioned cell can begin later tests with different internal states. First-switch evidence.

This connects directly to the wake-up study without making the mechanisms identical. Wake-up can improve an observed switching response while initial stress can damage another part of a device. An acceptance procedure that discards the first cycles may remove precisely the event needed to explain future reliability. In our state description, both $P$ and $n$ can change during conditioning. A larger subsequent loop does not prove that the defect population improved.

The thermal-processing paper examines another history: fabrication heat after channel formation. Its capped oxide-channel FeFETs use an 8/3/8 nm HZO/Al₂O₃/HZO stack, with gallium- or tungsten-doped indium oxide channels. The authors report functional devices after particular 650 °C exposures, with different duration limits for the two channels. They also observe window changes as the process approaches channel failure. These are survival and electrical-response measurements after processing, not continuous 650 °C operating specifications. Process and measurement conditions.

A larger window near a failure boundary is an instructive warning about the choice of objective. If annealing shifts one threshold while simultaneously degrading channel conduction, maximizing window alone could select a poor process. The acceptable region must constrain both distinguishability and access current, as well as later retention and variation. Likewise, the 125 °C behavior in the wake-up study and the 650 °C process exposure in this study concern different experiments. A single “temperature robustness” score would erase the distinction.

The radiation study tests laminated polycrystalline-silicon-channel FeFETs under ionizing radiation. It reports retention of a large window and switching response through specified total-dose conditions, with state-dependent threshold shifts. Total ionizing dose measures accumulated ionization exposure; it does not describe every radiation failure mechanism. Its comparison supports the studied stack as a radiation-resilient storage candidate under the reported protocol. It does not qualify a controller, package, or complete storage system for all environments. Radiation experiment and scope.

The three stress studies are complementary probes of $F$ and $H$. Electrical history can create traps, fabrication heat can change channel structure, and radiation can change charge populations differently in different stored states. The fact that charge assists one operating mechanism does not make all charge-generation processes benign. A mechanism-based reliability claim must specify where charge appears and which state it destabilizes.

Combined stress can depend on order

Let $n$ now represent an illustrative normalized defect population solely for this example. Suppose an electrical operation changes it by $E(n)=n+a$, with increment $a$, while annealing leaves a fraction $r$ through $A(n)=rn$, where $0\le r\le1$. Anneal after electrical stress gives $A(E(n))=rn+ra$. Electrical stress after annealing gives $E(A(n))=rn+a$. Their difference is $(1-r)a$.

Starting from zero, with $a=1$ and $r=0.2$, the two sequences produce 0.2 and 1.0. This arithmetic does not model a specific selected device. It exposes the assumption hidden when separate qualification results are combined: stress operations need not commute. The useful follow-up is a controlled sequence experiment, such as cycling before versus after thermal exposure, with matched final read conditions. Passing two isolated tests does not establish the combined-use envelope.

8. AI changes the modeling workflow, not the evidence boundary

The two AI papers intervene at different locations in the research process. The retention-surrogate paper accelerates evaluation of an existing physical model. The agentic-calibration paper assists selection and calibration of physical models against measured transistor behavior. Confusing these jobs would obscure the strongest connection to the earlier papers: the first addresses computational cost, while the second also confronts model ambiguity.

A surrogate is a computational approximation to a reference mapping. In the retention work, a physics-informed neural operator learns simulated fields and retention-related behavior for a single FeFET configuration family. Physical relations enter the training objective; the reference data come from TCAD, technology computer-aided design. The reported sweep axes include HZO thickness, temperature, and retention time. This supports rapid exploration within the investigated modeling problem. It does not supply a measured many-year lifetime or a full-array retention demonstration. Training scope and Table I.

The paper reports approximately 60 hours for a full TCAD sweep versus about 10 seconds for surrogate evaluation, on different stated computing platforms. Those are authors' workflow timings. They should not be described as a hardware-independent algorithmic speedup, nor as a total project cost including all training-data generation and model development. To decide when a surrogate saves resources, the running memory study needs the one-time preparation cost and the number of future evaluations. Computational comparison.

Let $C_0$ be one-time preparation cost, $c_T$ the cost per direct simulation, $c_S$ the cost per surrogate query, and $N$ the number of comparable queries. Measure all costs in one declared unit, such as machine-hours on a specified resource-equivalent basis. Direct cost is $Nc_T$; surrogate cost is $C_0+Nc_S$. If $c_T\gt c_S$, the surrogate is cheaper when

$$N\gt\frac{C_0}{c_T-c_S}.$$

For illustrative costs of 100 machine-hours preparation, 1 hour per direct query, and 0.001 hour per surrogate query, more than 100.10 queries are needed; the first cheaper integer case is 101. These are not the paper's cost measurements. They show why a dramatic inference-time ratio can be valuable for repeated design exploration but irrelevant to a one-off calculation. Model retraining after a process change can alter the break-even point.

The second issue is inherited error. Let $f_R$ denote the real-device response, $f_T$ the TCAD response, and $f_S$ the surrogate response for a common test input. For a common scalar observable or chosen norm, the triangle inequality gives

$$\|f_S-f_R\|\le\|f_S-f_T\|+\|f_T-f_R\|.$$

Small surrogate error against TCAD controls only the first term. If the second is unknown, accuracy against devices remains unbounded by that comparison. For example, a 5 mV surrogate error and a separately established 40 mV simulator error give a 45 mV upper bound by this inequality, not a 5 mV bound. Physics-informed training can help enforce chosen constraints; it cannot guarantee that omitted physical mechanisms are correct.

The agentic work addresses the reference model itself. Its language-model agent proposes bounded changes based on measurement residuals and supporting information; a human reviews the modeling setup and proposed updates; Sentaurus executes the physics. For a bottom-gate tungsten-doped indium-oxide transistor, the authors report improvement of a multi-metric fitting objective and tests across additional drain biases and channel lengths. Those held-out tests ask whether a fixed calibrated model transfers beyond the fitted conditions. They do not prove unique microscopic identification or autonomous discovery without human review. Workflow, responsibilities, and validation design.

The relationship to the 2021 extraction paper is now visible. Both use numerical fitting to turn electrical observations into models. The later work adds model-class choices, richer transistor physics, orchestration, and explicit transfer tests. The persistent difficulty is still identifiability. A faster search through models can find plausible alternatives more efficiently, but it cannot create information that the experiment did not contain.

A valuable next step would combine these roles: experimentally constrain a device model, train a cheaper approximation within a declared domain, and use it to choose experiments where competing models disagree. That is a proposed research workflow, not a demonstrated integration of the two papers. It also explains the limited meaning of corporate collaboration: coauthorship by researchers at semiconductor and AI companies establishes collaboration on these studies, not adoption of the memory stack in a shipping accelerator.

9. What reaches an AI system?

The phrase “memory for AI” hides several distinct roles. Nonvolatile storage retains model files or checkpoints without power. Working memory holds actively accessed weights and temporary state. A cache stores a selected subset close to compute. Compute-in-memory performs some arithmetic through memory-associated operations. A technology can be promising for one role without satisfying the latency, endurance, bandwidth, or precision requirements of the others.

The vertical-NAND discussion most directly motivates dense nonvolatile storage. A proposed system benefit could arise from capacity, write cost, loading behavior, or storage reliability. Moving from that role to working memory for token-by-token inference requires another architectural argument. It must explain access granularity, interfaces, sustained bandwidth, read disturbance, and how repeated updates affect endurance. The selected papers do not jointly demonstrate that transition.

The running memory case therefore needs a fixed application contract before optimization. Specify usable capacity after redundancy, target error probability, read/write traffic, retention interval, temperature history, and the portion of the workload that accesses the new memory. A checkpoint store and a frequently updated attention cache impose different contracts. Without that choice, an improvement in a device metric can be presented as a benefit to an application that rarely uses the improved operation.

A simple accounting identity makes the limitation quantitative. Let $f$ be the fraction of baseline energy attributable to the component actually improved, and let $\eta$ be the fractional reduction in that component's energy, with both between zero and one. Assume unchanged workload, runtime, all other energy terms, and no added overhead. Then the total energy ratio is $1-f\eta$. If the improved operation is 2% of total energy and becomes 50% cheaper, total energy falls by 1%. The calculation is illustrative and says nothing about the actual energy share of the selected devices.

Architecture changes can produce larger effects by altering data movement, parallelism, or capacity constraints, but they also change the comparison. A capacity gain that permits a larger batch is a redesign benefit, not the same claim as lower cell-switching energy at fixed batch. The course capstone deliberately distinguishes fixed-architecture sensitivity from redesign and marks its device-to-system couplings as assumptions. This research synthesis supplies the physical questions those assumptions must eventually answer.

The promising trend in the selected corpus is therefore more precise than a claim of imminent AI-hardware replacement. Materials and device research increasingly intersects with architecture-aware reliability and AI-assisted model development. That combination can make design exploration more informative. Whether it improves useful computation depends on an experimentally supported interface between those levels, which remains a research task.

10. Overlooked questions and discriminating tests

True unknown unknowns cannot be exhaustively listed in advance. What we can expose are omitted variables and assumptions that a reader might not realize are consequential. The following questions arise from connecting the selected works; they are proposed tests and failure hypotheses, not claims that the cited authors overlooked or failed them.

The strongest results may not coexist

A favorable radiation result on a laminated polycrystalline-silicon device, a favorable thermal result on a capped oxide-channel device, and a favorable capacitor wake-up result do not define one demonstrated stack. Combining their best numbers creates a hypothetical technology. The missing evidence is joint compatibility: fabricate the intended combination and measure all relevant constraints on it. A process split that improves one constraint but removes the others is still informative because it identifies a tradeoff rather than a universal improvement.

Mean improvement can conceal array-level failure

A larger mean window does not bound the fraction of cells in the tails. Grain-to-grain variation, spatial process gradients, and shared bias disturbance can correlate failures. Error-correcting codes depend on the distribution of errors within protected words, not merely the mean error probability across a wafer. The discriminating experiment records location and repeated history, retaining device failures and reporting joint statistics rather than only average successful traces. The correlated-error lesson shows why equal marginal error rates can produce different word-failure rates.

Conditioning and sample selection can change the population

If measurements include only devices that survive a conditioning sequence, the reported later distribution describes survivors. It does not describe yield from all fabricated cells. The first-switch and wake-up papers make this distinction consequential. Track devices from pristine state, record the conditioning protocol, and count rejected devices with reasons. Compare a population-level objective that includes survival and readable states. Otherwise, a procedure can appear to improve reliability by deleting the devices that revealed its cost.

A screening mechanism must survive the right time scale

Charge that helps during a programming pulse may relax before a later read. Charge stable during retention may respond too slowly for fast programming. These are different constraints on a distribution of trapping and detrapping times. A single exponential is a useful teaching model but may hide several mechanisms. Pulse-width sweeps followed by logarithmically spaced read delays at multiple temperatures can distinguish fast screening from slower stabilization. The experiment should preserve the same device and programming definition so that changes in the apparent state are interpretable.

Process sequence can matter as much as final composition

The oxidant, oxygen-reservoir, and thermal papers together suggest that an interface is not fully described by its final chemical label. Annealing before versus after channel capping can change diffusion and defect equilibria. Similar final average composition can conceal different local configurations. Compare matched process sequences with chemical and electrical characterization at intermediate stages. This turns a vague “integration problem” into a question about which state transformations depend on order.

An unselected cell is still part of the experiment

Vertical strings require biases on cells other than the one being accessed. A mechanism that improves selected-cell screening might also alter the response to repeated partial stresses. Single-cell retention under zero bias does not establish retention during neighboring reads and writes. Test worst-case data patterns and repeated unselected-cell biases, then assess state distributions. This is an architectural consequence of the sensing and NAND perspectives, not a claim that every selected materials paper should already contain a complete array qualification.

Thermal and radiation robustness are multidimensional

Anneal survival, hot operation, hot retention, cumulative dose, and single-event response are different axes. Combining them into one “robustness” label invites an invalid application claim. Declare the intended environment first and identify which stress mechanisms the source actually tests. Then add sequence and combined-stress measurements where the mechanism suggests interaction. A candidate for an extreme-environment storage cell still needs a compatible peripheral circuit and package.

A shared simulator can create shared blind spots

A surrogate trained on one solver and an agent judged by improvement in that solver can agree strongly while sharing an omitted mechanism. Their agreement is not independent corroboration. An experiment outside the calibration set, or a genuinely different physical observable, supplies a stronger test. Validation should retain solver nonconvergence and rejected updates as outcomes: ignoring them can make a modeling workflow appear more reliable than the process a researcher must actually use.

Optimization finds regions where a model is weakest

A surrogate may be accurate around its training samples yet inaccurate at an extreme point selected by optimization. The optimizer systematically seeks favorable predictions, including favorable errors. Reserve a high-fidelity validation budget for proposed optima, examine distance from the training domain, and use uncertainty or trust-region restrictions where justified. A smooth, physics-regularized prediction is not proof that an unexplored geometry is physically realizable.

A better fit is not automatically a better explanation

Adding model terms can reduce residuals even when the extra terms are not uniquely supported. Compare competing explanations on observations withheld by physical condition, not only random points from the same smooth curve. Bias, geometry, time, and temperature challenge different dependencies. For the running case, the next experiment should separate a polarization-driven retained window from a transient charge-driven window. Improving the precision of the initial window measurement alone may leave that decision unresolved.

These questions change the research priority. Once a stack has a sufficiently large nominal signal, distribution control and mechanistic discrimination may be more valuable than another increase in the best-case window. That is a conditional design judgment derived from the application constraints. It should be revised if measurements show that nominal signal remains the binding limitation.

11. A connected research agenda

The fifteen works support a coherent hypothesis for future investigation: useful ferroelectric memory requires co-design of structural order, interfacial charge dynamics, the semiconductor channel, and the sensing protocol. Each paper constrains part of this hypothesis. None alone closes the chain from material to array to application. The most informative next work connects those parts on the same specimens and under a common operating contract.

Begin with paired measurements of polarization-sensitive response and transistor threshold distributions. Fix geometry, pulse history, delay, and temperature. Compare competing models that attribute the window to different mixtures of polarization and charge, using the early extraction and screening studies as motivation. Freeze fitted models before testing new delays or temperatures. This establishes whether the proposed design variable has a stable meaning outside the curve used to identify it.

Next, use controlled process interventions drawn from the interlayer and thermal studies. Change one accessible process factor while recording the other properties it alters. The goal is not necessarily to isolate a single microscopic variable perfectly, which may be impossible. It is to find a reproducible intervention whose joint consequences support the application contract. Include pristine, conditioned, stressed, and failed devices so that yield and reliability remain connected.

Then choose the sensing architecture explicitly. Calculate required adjacent-state margins from measured distributions, characterize disturbance, and include references and peripheral overhead. If the architecture needs more levels than the data support, stop claiming the nominal density and investigate a different read scheme or fewer levels. The architecture review and NAND perspective guide these alternatives; the domain and BaTiO₃ studies keep other physical routes available without treating them as drop-in replacements.

Finally, use calibrated models and validated surrogates to allocate experimental effort. A useful active-learning criterion is disagreement between physically plausible models on a decision-relevant observable. Choose an experiment where the models predict different retained margins and where either result would change the architecture choice. The agentic workflow can help organize proposals and simulator tests, but experimental validity remains a separate obligation.

For an independent computational project, a feasible contribution is a compatibility-aware evidence map and reproducible uncertainty analysis, rather than an unsupported attempt to build a complete proprietary process model. Represent each published result with its material stack, geometry, history, measurement, and uncertainty. Ask which combinations of claims are jointly demonstrated and which require new experiments. That project connects scientific reading, causal reasoning, device physics, and AI-assisted research without inventing missing calibration data.

The central achievement of this selected portfolio is a richer specification of what must be controlled and modeled. Polarization remains essential, but a useful memory state is a property of the interacting device and its history. The course's foundations, circuits, and systems chapters provide the tools to examine that statement quantitatively; the selected research provides the concrete scientific questions.

12. Worked exercises and reproducible checks

The companion calculation script uses Python's standard library and writes the saved illustrative results. It checks the retention crossover, multilevel Gaussian error, stress-order example, surrogate-cost break-even, and energy accounting. Inputs are the explicitly assumed numbers above; outputs are teaching calculations. It reads no measured data, calls no external service, and overwrites only its named result file. Invalid widths, level counts, and costs raise an error.

1. Derive what one threshold measurement cannot identify

Problem. With $\delta V_{\mathrm{th}}=10\delta P+10\delta q=0.1$ V in the stated units, find the full family of compatible perturbations. Would repeating the same measurement at ten times the precision identify the mechanism?

Solution. Rearranging gives $\delta q=0.01-\delta P$. Every point on this line has the same predicted observation. Lower measurement noise narrows uncertainty perpendicular to the line but does not remove the unobserved direction along it. A second protocol must have nonproportional sensitivities to polarization and charge, together with a justified observation model. Precision and identifiability are different requirements.

2. Find the delay that reverses a material ranking

Problem. For the two hypothetical windows in Section 4, derive the crossover and decide whether a 1 ms read supports a claim about a 1,000 s read.

Solution. Set $0.6+0.6e^{-t/100}=0.9$. Subtract 0.6 and divide by 0.6 to obtain $e^{-t/100}=1/2$, so $t=100\ln2=69.3147$ s. At 1 ms, stack A is approximately 1.199994 V; at 1,000 s, it is 0.600027 V. The short-delay ranking reverses. A long-delay claim requires a model supported over that interval or the corresponding measurement, not an initial-window comparison.

3. Explain why doubling the number of levels is costly

Problem. With total span 1.2 V and width 0.05 V, compare four and eight equally spaced levels. Identify the physical quantity that must improve if the span cannot increase.

Solution. Four levels have adjacent separation 0.4 V and normalized half-margin 4. Eight levels have separation $1.2/7$ V and half-margin $12/7$. Weighting the one-sided errors of the outer states and the two-sided errors of interior states gives approximately $4.75\times10^{-5}$ and 0.0757 symbol error. If span is fixed, the widths must shrink, or the distribution/read model and error-correction strategy must change in a justified way. More levels cannot be inferred from a large binary window alone.

4. Diagnose the impossible composite device

Problem. A proposal combines BaTiO₃ switching voltage, zirconia domain footprint, HZO interlayer retention, and oxide-channel anneal survival into one table labeled “demonstrated memory.” What is wrong, and how can the table become useful?

Solution. The rows describe different specimens, structures, observables, and protocols. Their conjunction has not been demonstrated. Relabel the table as candidate mechanisms and constraints, preserve each source's conditions, and add a compatibility column identifying the experiments needed to combine them. The result can guide research, but it cannot serve as a measured specification for one device.

5. Design a test that exposes stress-order dependence

Problem. Use $E(n)=n+1$ and $A(n)=0.2n$ to compare two histories starting from zero. Describe the control needed in a physical experiment.

Solution. Electrical stress followed by annealing gives $A(E(0))=0.2$. Annealing followed by electrical stress gives $E(A(0))=1$. Compare matched device populations with the same stress amplitudes, durations, final temperature, and read delay; change only sequence. Include unstressed and singly stressed controls to separate interaction from baseline differences. The toy calculation establishes that order dependence is possible, not its magnitude in a real stack.

6. Separate a fast surrogate from an accurate device prediction

Problem. A surrogate differs from TCAD by at most 5 mV. No experimental bound on TCAD error is available. What accuracy claim follows? Then assume a valid 40 mV bound for the same test domain.

Solution. Initially, only a 5 mV agreement claim with the reference model follows. The unknown TCAD-to-device error prevents a finite device-accuracy bound from that fact alone. With the additional 40 mV bound, the triangle inequality gives at most 45 mV. Both bounds must concern the same observable and domain; an average error cannot silently substitute for a worst-case bound.

7. Calculate whether a modeling workflow is worth preparing

Problem. Use the illustrative 100-hour preparation, 1-hour direct query, and 0.001-hour surrogate query. Compare 100 and 101 queries. What could invalidate the comparison?

Solution. At 100 queries the direct cost is 100 hours and the surrogate total is 100.1 hours. At 101, the totals are 101 and 100.101 hours. The crossover is therefore 101 integer queries. Retraining, unequal query scope, differing hardware accounting, validation overhead, or changing the physical model can invalidate the fixed-cost comparison. Report these costs when evaluating a real workflow.

8. Choose a next experiment that can change the decision

Problem. A process split raises initial mean window by 30%, but late-time distributions and full-access energy are unknown. The intended application needs multilevel retention. Should the next experiment refine the initial mean, measure late-time distributions, or benchmark an unrelated AI workload?

Solution. Measure the retained distributions under the application's declared histories, preferably alongside observables that distinguish polarization and trapped charge. This can reveal whether the extra window survives and whether distribution tails consume it. Refining the initial mean may leave the binding uncertainty unchanged. An unrelated workload cannot repair the missing device-to-architecture link. If retention passes, measure disturbance and complete access cost before drawing a system-energy conclusion.
