The expanded textbook edition

The first edition was too compressed to support the intended depth of learning. All ten chapters and the capstone have been expanded using the teaching standard of the stronger Stanford AA203 and CMU AI Agents study chapters: develop a consequential example, derive the mechanism, test it numerically, examine a failure case, and work through the engineering consequence.

The revised edition contains approximately 50,000 words and 82 expandable worked solutions, plus worked examples within the exposition. The same counter gives about 21,100 words for the first edition. These counts exclude navigation but include mathematical notation, so they are depth diagnostics rather than a precise count of prose. More important additions include exact electrostatics, domain-wall energetics, inverse-model uncertainty, correlated errors, controller timing, thermal coupling, attention traffic, queueing tails, and robust cross-stack decisions.

What changes in the learning experience

Each chapter now has a complete contents list with links to its sections. The original baseline projects remain, and a new cumulative depth laboratory supplies independent checks and assumption-changing experiments. Definitions, units, operating conditions, and limits accompany the central equations. Exercises ask the reader to derive, compute, diagnose, and design, rather than only recall a definition.

Start with the exact electrostatics treatment, compare two experimental designs with a hundredfold difference in parameter uncertainty, then follow correlated errors into ECC. At system scale, the distinction between mean and p99 latency changes the feasible utilization, and joint uncertainty changes the material margin required by the capstone.

Scientific grounding and limits

The course is grounded in acquired research papers and established theory. No verified Khan lecture transcript was available, so the transcript-grounded-textbook procedure was adapted to a paper-based source map. The revision evidence map and scientific claim ledger distinguish source observations, author interpretations, derivations, teaching assumptions, and executed checks.

The new numerical experiments are synthetic and reproducible. Their checks include independent quadrature, analytical limits, exact finite enumeration, conservation, and rejection of invalid inputs. The original Python, SPICE, RTL, and generic synthesized-controller checks are retained. Scientific editing is a separate pass by the same authoring agent, not independent expert peer review. The device model remains illustrative and the physical macro remains uncharacterized.

The next research step is calibration against matched measured device data and operating histories. More detailed explanations make the evidence boundary easier to inspect; they do not supply unavailable model cards, a research-device PDK, or an end-to-end product demonstration.
