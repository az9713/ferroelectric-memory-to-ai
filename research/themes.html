Research themes and unresolved questions

The organizing thread is control and interpretation of polarization in thin-film devices, followed by the problem of sensing, preserving, and exploiting the resulting state. This is a synthesis of selected publications, not an attribution of individual contributions from author order. Parallel collaborations and themes overlap; the table is not a claim that one project caused the next.

What has evolved?

The selected 2021–23 work emphasizes extraction, electrostatics, variation, and stability. Later sources increasingly connect engineered stacks and oxide channels to three-dimensional storage and reliability constraints. The 2025–26 architecture papers broaden the sensing-to-system perspective, while 2026 modeling papers add AI tools to calibration and surrogate construction. Older themes continue; a publication date is not a sharp change of research direction.

The strongest questions for a new reader

Ask whether the paper measures polarization, displacement, current, or an inferred state; whether nominal improvement survives distribution tails; whether the controlling variable is independently changed; and whether a circuit or system result uses the same device conditions. “Smaller domain,” “larger window,” “faster simulation,” and “lower device voltage” describe different achievements. None alone establishes lower facility energy.

Competing technologies should be compared by role. SRAM competes for low-latency cache behavior; DRAM/HBM for working-memory capacity and bandwidth; charge-trap NAND for persistent storage; MRAM, ReRAM, PCM, and FeFET variants for particular combinations of retention, write cost, density, endurance, and integration. This course does not assign context-free winner rankings or unsourced performance constants.

The most promising solo research extension is a model-discrimination benchmark: take two mechanisms that fit the same curve, select a new bias/temperature/pulse condition that maximizes their disagreement, and test whether the added observation resolves the ambiguity. It joins device physics, inverse problems, uncertainty, and experimental design without requiring a fabrication claim.
