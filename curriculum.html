Curriculum and prerequisite map

Prof. Asif Khan: connected research synthesis · Dedicated bibliography.

The bounded course contains ten chapters plus a capstone. Plan roughly 116–160 hours for reading, derivations, exercises, and baseline project work. Optional extensions can add substantially more. At six hours per week that is about 19–27 weeks; at ten, about 12–16 weeks. These are planning estimates, not a required schedule.

Your mathematical maturity is assumed. Current familiarity with semiconductor processing, Python, HDL semantics, transformer serving, or EDA tools is not assumed. Read the software and RTL lessons before modifying the corresponding implementation. If you already know a topic, demonstrate it through the diagnostic or an exercise rather than rereading every paragraph.

Hidden prerequisites worth making explicit

Experimental: distinguish instrument voltage from internal field, current integration from polarization, a reset protocol from a pristine state, and a selected device from a distribution. Numerical: use consistent units, preserve random seeds, distinguish interpolation from validation, inspect optimizer bounds, and test limiting cases. Digital: understand nonblocking assignments, cycle boundaries, reset semantics, and simulation-only state. Systems: specify workload quality, context, averaging interval, and accounting boundary before comparing energy.

A productive study rhythm

For each chapter, answer its consequential question in your own words, derive one central relation without copying, run the project, change one assumption, and explain a failure case. Attempt exercises before reading their open solutions. Use the guided papers after learning the mechanism; return to the original figures and identify which observation supports each claim.

Core work comprises the chapter and baseline project. The expanded core now develops experimental design, correlated errors, thermal networks, and queueing tails. Further implementation extensions include full minor-loop operators, event-driven serving simulation, real kernel profiling, and process-supported conventional physical design. They are follow-on research projects, not silently claimed completed deliverables.
